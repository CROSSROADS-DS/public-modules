# Activity Guide

## Your mission

Imagine that Capital Bikeshare has asked your team to examine trip data from Washington, DC. Your goal is to develop insights about how the service is currently being used. These insights could help the organization review its service and consider how it might better serve the community.

## Begin with questions

Start by developing one or more questions that could be useful to Capital Bikeshare. Your questions might involve location, time of day, weekdays and weekends, rider type, bike type, trip duration, or relationships among these attributes.

After meeting the dataset, you will decide which questions it can support and refine, replace, or add questions as needed.

## Shared investigation

The class will follow one selected investigation path together:

> What do the locations and timing of trips suggest about how Capital Bikeshare is currently serving Washington, DC?

You will examine the geographic reach of the represented network, activity across central Washington, daily timing, weekday and weekend differences, and selected combinations of place and time. At each step, the goal is to use evidence to answer a question—not simply to produce a visualization.

## How you will work

The notebook provides short Python helper-function calls. Before each call, the notebook explains its purpose. After it runs, you will examine the output, record observations, cite evidence, and consider what the result does and does not establish.

The guided activity prepares you to adjust helper-function options during further exploration.

## Optional data science extension

An optional clustering activity groups central-Washington stations by their hourly trip activity. The result provides an accessible introduction to unsupervised learning while emphasizing that algorithmic groups must be interpreted carefully.

## Further exploration

A complete exploration path compares member and casual riders to help Capital Bikeshare better understand different customer groups. You may use it as an in-class extension or assignment.

Your final response should communicate:

- the question you investigated;
- the evidence you examined;
- your main insight;
- a possible service implication;
- an important limitation; and
- additional information that would strengthen the analysis.
