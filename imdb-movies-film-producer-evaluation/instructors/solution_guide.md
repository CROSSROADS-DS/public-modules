# Solution Guide: Movies - Investigating Patterns, Types of Data, and Distribution of Data

This guide summarizes expected findings and discussion points. Student responses may differ; analysis is subjective, and this is perfectly acceptable when supported by data.

## Dataset evaluation

The dataset supports questions involving genre, critical score, parental rating, release date, financial success, and actor/director. It does not directly support conclusions about movies' themes, societal impact, details about franchise movies, or awards won.

Students should be aware that the dataset is not adjusted for inflation.

### Question Development

This will vary for every student. Students should be looking to make questions like "How do I make the best comedy?", "Are action movies or horror thrillers better?", "Who is the most popular director?"

### Question Refinement

This depends on students' questions. Examples include "I can't ask the question about K-dramas because there aren't enough Korean movies, so I'll ask about Japanese movies instead" and "The most recent movie was from 2019, so I can't ask about movies after the 2023 writers strike"


## Shared investigation

Stations are broadly distributed throughout the displayed central-Washington area. The map suggests substantial station presence, but it does not show whether every neighborhood or community has equal access, sufficient capacity, or reliable bicycle and dock availability.

### Genre Distribution

Some genres are simply less common out in the world than others. The popular genres are all very broad (action, comedy, adventure) categories that could fit many movies, while the unpopular genres are very specific. iMDB might use the more broad category when a movie could be one or the other (e.g. horror/thriller).

It's important to emphasize to students that any of these explanations are valid, but they are all conjecture, and so shouldn't be relied on. Students should use cautious language such as "might", "could", etc.

### Grouping, Shortlist, Removing small genres

Students should mention that having a few categories is simpler. Students ideally mention the issue of overlapping genres, such as Horror and Thriller, having to have their movies placed in one.

### Hypothesis of Drama & Comedy's ratings

This is a subjective hypothesis; any answer is okay as long as students justify their response.

### Summary statistic analysis

Students may notice that drama and comedy movies have a comparable average gross income, but the median for comedy movies is far higher. A strong response will consider this further and deduce that there are possibly a few comedy movies making very low money, dragging its average down.

### Chart analysis

Student should mention that drama usually is higher than comedy. Student should mention the lines touching at the "present day" end of the chart. Student should have an answer for patterns, observations, and projections, but this is subjective and their answers may be different.

## Pitching (long-response)

This will vary; either can be justified. Example concept: "When pitching this movie, I should make it sound dramatic and play up the drama side because drama movies have a high critical score, and while currently they might be equal average scores, there is no exchange of first place in the past, so it's unlikely that will be a continuing trend. Drama movies are also increasing in their average expected return, while comedy movies are decreasing. This trend may be continuing because pre-2010s, there are many moments when each genre makes more money or makes less: a return to form is very possible."
