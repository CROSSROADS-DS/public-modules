# Activity Guide: Movies - Investigating Patterns, Types of Data, and Distribution of Data

## Your job

Imagine that you are a movie producer, and you're determining how to pitch a movie. Data-driven insights are very convincing in a creative field since you can directly point to a business impact for the studio. Your goal is to use some provided data and develop insights that can be used to convince a studio your movie is worth producing. 

## Begin with questions

Start by developing one or more questions that could be useful to your investigation. Your questions might involve genre, parental rating, critical reception, commercial return, or relationships among these attributes.

After viewing the dataset, you will decide which questions it can support and refine, replace, or add questions as needed.

## Shared investigation

The class will follow one selected investigation path together:

**What do the ratings of different genres suggest about the appeal of films within that genre?**

You will examine the breadth of genres available in the dataset and investigate the difference between two genres, using summary statistics and graphical charts. At each step, the goal is to use the evidence to answer a question, not simply to look at a visualization.

## How you will work

The notebook provides short Python helper-function calls. Before each call, the notebook explains its purpose. After it runs, you will examine the output, record observations, cite evidence, and consider what the result does and does not establish.

The guided activity prepares you to adjust helper-function options during further exploration.

## Optional independent extension

An optional independent activity allows you to investigate separate pairs of genre and see the difference between more than just the provided genres. The result allows for interpretation of independently chosen categories.

## Dataset evaluation

The dataset supports questions involving genre, critical score, parental rating, release date, financial success, and actor/director. It does not directly support conclusions about movies' themes, societal impact, details about franchise movies, or awards won.

Students should be aware that the dataset is not adjusted for inflation.

## Acceptable variation in responses

Students may emphasize implications, but strong responses should:

- cite visible or tabular evidence;
- use cautious language such as “suggests,” “is consistent with,” or “may”;
- avoid treating interpretations as recorded facts;
- connect the finding to a producer's job; and
- identify at least one limitation in the data that matters for the claim.
