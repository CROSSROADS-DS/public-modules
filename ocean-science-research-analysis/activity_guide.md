# Investigating ocean science research: Who, Where, When?

## Your mission

Imagine that **you are in charge of a country's ocean science policy**. **Your goal is to understand your country's ocean science academic publication output to inform research funding policy decisions**. Your country wants to invest more funding in addressing [United Nations Sustainability Development Goals](https://sdgs.un.org/goals) (SDG), particularly [SDG 14 Life Below Water](https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal14). Your work will provide insights for your government into the strengths and weaknesses of your country's ocean science research, directing future policy.

## Begin with questions

Start by developing one or more questions that could be useful for your government. Your questions might involve the number and year of publications, as well as number of citations or the authoring countries, or relationships among these (or other) attributes.

After meeting the dataset, you will decide which questions it can support and refine, replace, or add questions as needed.

## Shared investigation

The class will follow one selected investigation path together:

    You are Canada's ocean science policy manager and must understand Canada's recent publication output to formulate funding policy recommendations


You will examine **Canada's** publication output temporally and by ocean, comparing this to other countries. At each step, the goal is to use evidence to answer a question—not simply to produce a visualization.

## How you will work

The notebook provides short Python helper-function calls. Before each call, the notebook explains its purpose. After it runs, you will examine the output, record observations, cite evidence, and consider what the result does and does not establish.

The guided activity prepares you to adjust helper-function options during further exploration.

## Concluding the analysis

After completing the analysis, you will be asked to
- State 3-5 important results from your analysis
- Based on these, suggest 2-4 policy funding recommendations for **Canada's** ocean science program
- Based on the above, state any limitations of the analysis and/or other data you would want to analyze to craft better policy recommendations.

This will help you synthesize your results, selecting the most important and relevant to frame your policy recommendations. 

## Optional data science extension

An optional independent extension allows you to select a country of your own choosing and carry out the analysis. Explain your choice and present hypotheses before analyzing the data. Revisit these after the analysis to see if these were correct.
