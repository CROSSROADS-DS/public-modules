# Investigating ocean science research: Who, Where, When?

## Module overview
How can academic publication data help a government formulate its science policy and funding decisions? In this introductory module, students take the role of a government minister who wants to understand their country's ocean science output to guide decision making.

Students begin by developing questions that could be useful to the government. They then evaluate the available data, refine their questions, and follow a shared investigation of the data for a particular country, **Canada**. The activity emphasizes evidence-based reasoning, cautious interpretation, and the idea that limited or unexpected findings can still be informative.

## Intended audience

This module is designed for introductory college courses in data science, geography, earth science and environmental studies, economics, public policy, political science, business, and general education. It can also serve as an approachable early activity in a programming or statistics course.

No prior programming experience is required. Students run and adjust short helper-function calls while focusing on questions, evidence, and interpretation. Students with more experience may inspect or extend the Python code.

## Learning goals

By the end of the module, students should be able to:

- formulate and refine questions that can be investigated with available data;
- use temporal evidence to describe patterns;
- understand varying data visualization options; 
- distinguish observations from interpretations and implications;
- identify important limitations of a prepared dataset;
- use simple helper functions to continue an independent investigation; and
- explain how data science can support decisions about science policy.

## Data

The activity uses data from the **open-access academic article** ["Assessing the global ocean science community: understanding international collaboration, concerns and the current state of ocean basin research"](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44183-023-00020-y) published in the journal *npj Ocean Sustainability*. The dataset has been adapted for learning, interpretability, and reliable browser-based use and provides metadata for academic publications relevant to ocean science.

The module briefly explains the columns within the dataset. Due to data formatting, each row does not necessarily equate to one publication's complete metadata.

## Classroom use

The guided investigation is designed for approximately **60 minutes**. Instructors may add the optional independent exploration for a **90 minute** session, homework, or follow-up assignment.

The notebook is modular. Instructors may use the shared investigation alone, assign the independent exploration, or select individual sections for a shorter class.
